Equipment light cheat sheet

Exact meanings vary by manufacturer. Always check your own manual.

What you seeCommonly meansFirst thing to try
No lights at allNo powerCheck the outlet, power strip and adapter.
Power on, internet light off or red/orangeNo incoming connectionCheck cables, restart in order, then contact your provider if it persists.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between a modem and a router?

The modem connects to your service line. The router creates your home network and shares that connection. Some devices combine both.

Why is Wi-Fi slow upstairs?

Distance, floors and walls weaken the signal. Move the router to a central, higher location or add a wired access point or mesh node.

Should I buy my own equipment?

Owning compatible equipment can avoid rental fees, but check compatibility with your provider and weigh the upfront cost.

Is a faster plan the fix for slow Wi-Fi?

Not always. If wired tests reach your plan speed, the bottleneck is usually your Wi-Fi setup, not the plan.

Can TKBNet fix my outage or change my account?

We can troubleshoot your own equipment, but we cannot access provider systems. Outages, billing and account changes go through your provider. See Disclosures.

How do I protect my home network?

Use WPA2 or WPA3, change the default admin password, keep router firmware updated, and use a unique Wi-Fi password.

What should I do first in an outage?

Check power, read the equipment lights, test by Ethernet cable, restart in order, then check your provider’s status on your phone. See our outage help.

What is a mesh system?

A set of devices that work together to blanket a home in Wi-Fi, replacing a single router and extenders.

What is a good ping or latency?

Under about 50 ms is comfortable for calls and gaming on most connections; lower is better for competitive play.
