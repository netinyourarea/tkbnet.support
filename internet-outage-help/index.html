Internet & Wi-Fi Outage Help

Is it your equipment or an outage on your provider’s network? Work it out in minutes, and know exactly what to do next.

Outage triage

Is it you, or your provider?

Answer four quick questions. This is a general guide, not a diagnosis, and nothing you enter leaves your device.

Tip: check your provider’s status page or app from your phone using mobile data, since your home Wi-Fi may be down.

1. Are ALL devices offline, including one connected to the router with an Ethernet cable?
2. Is the internet (or WAN/globe) light on your modem or router off, red or orange?
3. Does your provider or a neighbor report an outage in your area?
4. Do SOME devices still get internet on the same Wi-Fi?
Answer the questions to see a suggestion.

Local or provider?

How to tell the difference

Everything offline, but the internet light looks normalRouter or Wi-Fi fault, or an upstream issueTry a wired test, restart in order, check status.
Wired works, Wi-Fi does notRouter wireless problemRestart router, then check Wi-Fi settings. Not an outage.

Do this first

Five-minute outage checklist

  1. 1

    Check the power

    Confirm modem and router are plugged in and the outlet works. A tripped breaker or loose adapter is surprisingly common.

  2. 2

    Read the lights

    Photograph them. A warning color on the internet light usually points to the line or an outage.

  3. 3

    Test by cable

    Plug a laptop into the router by Ethernet. This separates Wi-Fi faults from a connection fault.

  4. 4

    Restart in order

    Modem first, then router, waiting 2 to 3 minutes each. See the restart checklist.

  5. 5

    Check status on mobile data

    Open your provider’s app or website on your phone, or call their outage line, and note any reported outage.

During an outage

Stay connected and protected

  • Use your phone as a hotspot for essential tasks, if your plan allows it.
  • Do not factory-reset your router. It rarely helps and can erase your settings.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics during storms or power surges.
  • Write down when the outage started and each symptom. Your provider will ask.
  • Save phone battery. Mobile networks may be busier than usual.

After it is back

Bring everything back cleanly

  • Restart modem then router once service returns.
  • Wait 5 minutes before testing speed.
  • Toggle Wi-Fi on stuck devices or restart them.
  • Re-link smart-home devices in their apps if needed.
  • If drops continue, keep a log and report them to your provider.

Be ready next time

Reduce the impact of future outages

Power protection

A small battery backup (UPS) keeps your modem and router alive through brief power blips.

Backup connection

Know how to use your phone hotspot, and check if your plan includes it.

Keep your records

Save your account number, equipment model and your provider’s outage line somewhere offline.

FAQ

Outage questions

How long do internet outages last?

It depends on the cause. Brief ones may clear in under an hour, while storm damage or line repairs can take longer. Your provider is the source for restoration times.

Can I get a credit for an outage?

Some providers offer credits. Ask your provider and have your outage notes ready. We cannot request credits for you.

Why is my Wi-Fi down but the internet light is fine?

The router’s wireless side may have failed or hung. Restart the router and test by Ethernet to confirm.

Should I call my provider or TKBNet?

If your provider reports an outage, wait for them. If you cannot tell, or the outage seems to be in your home, we can help you work it out. We do not replace your provider.

Does TKBNet serve my state?

Yes. Our phone support covers households anywhere in the USA.
