The basics

Wi-Fi in two minutes

Modem

Connects to your service line and brings internet into your home. On fiber this is an optical terminal.

Router

Creates your home network and shares the connection by Wi-Fi or cable. Many homes have a combined unit.

Your devices

Phones, laptops and TVs all share the router’s capacity. More devices means less for each.

Wi-Fi bandRangeSpeedBest for
2.4 GHzLongestLowerFar rooms, smart-home gear, basic browsing
5 GHzMediumHighStreaming, video calls, gaming
6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E/7)ShortestHighestClose-range, high-demand devices

Router placement

  • Place it high and central, in the open, not inside a cabinet or behind a TV.
  • Keep it away from microwaves, cordless-phone bases, baby monitors, mirrors and big metal objects.
  • Orient external antennas one vertical, one horizontal.
  • For weak rooms, add a wired access point or mesh node about halfway.

Diagnose

Find out where the problem is

  1. 1

    One device or all of them?

    One device: forget the network on it, rejoin, restart it. All devices: continue.

  2. 2

    Wi-Fi or the connection itself?

    Plug a laptop into the router by Ethernet. Works by cable but not Wi-Fi: wireless problem. Slow or down by cable too: router or incoming service.

  3. 3

    Check the lights

    Your manual explains each light. A light meaning no signal or no internet usually points at the line, not your devices.

  4. 4

    Restart in order

    Follow the restart checklist: modem first, router second.

  5. 5

    Check cables

    Reseat every connector firmly and look for kinks or damage.

  6. 6

    Test at different times

    Slowness that clears late at night can point to local congestion.

Reading a speed test

Test by Ethernet cable with other devices idle, and run it a few times. Compare the result with the speed in your plan, remembering advertised speeds are usually "up to." Wired results far below your plan are worth raising with your provider, who can check the line and account.

Basic network security

  • Use WPA2 or WPA3 with a long, unique Wi-Fi password.
  • Change the default router admin password.
  • Keep router firmware updated.
  • Put visitors and smart devices on a guest network.

When it is a question for your provider

Outages, line faults, billing and account changes can only be resolved by the company that provides your service. We can help you work out what to ask.
